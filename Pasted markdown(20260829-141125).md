# 2. WHAT FIRMS ACTUALLY PROVIDES

NASA FIRMS is considerably more powerful than many teams will realize.

It provides active-fire/thermal-anomaly products from MODIS, VIIRS and Landsat, with historical and near-real-time access. VIIRS 375 m data are available from 2012 onward for S-NPP, 2018 onward for NOAA-20 and 2024 onward for NOAA-21; Landsat active-fire data are available from 2022. ([FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/content/active_fire/ "firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov"))

VIIRS 375 m has significantly finer spatial resolution than MODIS's roughly 1 km active-fire product and is better suited to smaller fires and improved perimeter mapping. ([MODAPS Services](https://modaps.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/services/about/products/viirs-land-c2-nrt/vnp14imgtdl_nrt.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "About | MODAPS Services"))

FIRMS also exposes fields such as:

- latitude/longitude
- acquisition date/time
- satellite
- instrument
- confidence
- brightness temperatures
- FRP
- day/night
- scan
- track

through its data services. ([FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/content/academy/data_api/firms_api_use.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "firms_api_use"))

### FIRMS is therefore excellent at:

**"There is a thermal anomaly here."**

It is **not automatically equivalent to:**

> "There is an accidental refinery fire here."

NASA itself warns that an active-fire detection may represent fire, hot smoke, agriculture or other sources, and that cloud cover can obscure detections. ([FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/map/?os=avdf\&utm_source=chatgpt.com "NASA | LANCE | FIRMS"))

# 3. THE BIG DISCOVERY: NASA ALREADY HAS STATIC THERMAL ANOMALIES

This is extremely important for your SIH strategy.

NASA's 2025 STA capability:

1. takes cumulative MODIS + VIIRS detections,
2. aggregates them spatially,
3. identifies frequently observed thermal activity,
4. filters it against authoritative industrial/natural-source datasets,
5. creates a static thermal anomaly mask,
6. dynamically associates current detections with that mask. ([Earthdata Wiki](https://wiki.earthdata.nasa.gov/spaces/FIRMS/blog/2025/02/28/425855667/FIRMS%2Bincorporates%2Bstatic%2Bthermal%2Banomalies%2Bdata%2Bto%2Bhelp%2Busers%2Bdifferentiate%2Bbetween%2Bvegetation%2Band%2Bnon%2Bvegetation%2Bfires. "FIRMS incorporates static thermal anomalies data to help users differentiate between vegetation and non vegetation fires. - Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS) - Earthdata Wiki"))

NASA describes this as a way of identifying industrial heat sources such as:

- gas flares
- cement plants
- steel plants
- petrochemical facilities
- mineral-processing facilities
- waste facilities. ([Earthdata Wiki](https://wiki.earthdata.nasa.gov/spaces/FIRMS/blog/2025/02/28/425855667/FIRMS%2Bincorporates%2Bstatic%2Bthermal%2Banomalies%2Bdata%2Bto%2Bhelp%2Busers%2Bdifferentiate%2Bbetween%2Bvegetation%2Band%2Bnon%2Bvegetation%2Bfires. # 8. THERMAL FINGERPRINTING

This idea is scientifically defensible.

Research has already explored identifying industrial heat sources using time series rather than relying only on a single thermal anomaly. One study specifically notes that individual industrial thermal anomalies can have substantial temporal heterogeneity and that time-series analysis can help characterize industrial heat sources. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425717304820?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Identifying industrial heat sources using time-series of the VIIRS Nightfire product with an object-oriented approach - ScienceDirect"))

Another study demonstrated industrial heat-source identification using multiple thermal anomaly datasets including VIIRS active fire, VIIRS Nightfire and Landsat-derived fire information. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/4/835?utm_source=chatgpt.com "A Model for Expressing Industrial Information Based on Object-Oriented Industrial Heat Sources Detected Using Multi-Source Thermal Anomaly Data in China"))

So we can legitimately build upon established remote-sensing research rather than inventing a questionable concept.

# M. FINAL VERDICT

# **YES. CHOOSE SIH26162.**

But I would make one strategic change to the way your team thinks about the PS:

> **Do not compete to build the best fire detector.**

NASA, ISRO and others already have extremely mature fire-detection capabilities. NASA FIRMS itself now has industrial/static thermal-anomaly functionality. ([Earthdata Wiki](https://wiki.earthdata.nasa.gov/spaces/FIRMS/blog/2025/02/28/425855667/FIRMS%2Bincorporates%2Bstatic%2Bthermal%2Banomalies%2Bdata%2Bto%2Bhelp%2Busers%2Bdifferentiate%2Bbetween%2Bvegetation%2Band%2Bnon%2Bvegetation%2Bfires. "FIRMS incorporates static thermal anomalies data to help users differentiate between vegetation and non vegetation fires. - Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS) - Earthdata Wiki"))

Instead:

# **Compete to build the best intelligence layer on top of satellite thermal detections.**

Your strongest narrative is:

> **"A satellite can tell us where heat exists. THERMOS tells us whether that heat is expected, what facility it belongs to, how its behavior has changed, why the change matters, and which events deserve investigation first."**

That is the version of **SIH26162 I would take to the Grand Finale.**

And I would make **one thing non-negotiable before you build the full product: prove the idea quantitatively with a small research prototype first — FIRMS-only vs spatial-context vs spatial+temporal facility intelligence.** If that experiment shows a meaningful reduction in false alarms / improvement in macro-F1 on unseen facilities, you have the evidence needed to turn this from a good SIH concept into a genuinely competitive one.
